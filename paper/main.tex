\title{CiO-Drone-Acoustic: Paired Reference-Microphone and Microphone-Array Recordings of Small Multirotor Aircraft Indoors, with Technical Validation}

\begin{abstract}
Acoustic detection and identification of small uncrewed aircraft are usually developed on clips from a single microphone, recorded with undocumented hardware, and evaluated with splits that let neighbouring seconds of one recording appear in both training and test data.
We release CiO-Drone-Acoustic, thirteen indoor takes of four DJI multirotors (Flip, Neo~2, Mini~4 Pro and Matrice~350), each captured simultaneously by a mono reference microphone (\SI{44.1}{\kilo\hertz}) and a ReSpeaker eight-slot microphone array (\SI{16}{\kilo\hertz}): \SI{49.7}{\minute} of synchronized audio per sensor, with three aircraft at sidecar distances of 2, 5 and \SI{10}{\meter}, an unguarded free flight, and two multi-aircraft free flights.
Beyond describing the deposit, we re-measure what a user of the files would otherwise assume.
Cross-correlation shows that nine \emph{synchronized} pairs are aligned to within \SI{65}{\milli\second} but the two chains drift apart by \SIrange{178}{368}{ppm}, while four pairs carry offsets of \SI{-3.59}{}, \SI{-2.11}{}, \SI{+0.40}{} and \SI{+1.90}{\second} that the container-length differences do not predict.
The array delivers at most five acoustic channels per take, and their position among the eight file slots rotates between takes, consistent with a documented driver fault.
Median level falls by only \SIrange{3.9}{5.3}{\decibel} from 2 to \SI{10}{\meter}, against \SI{14}{\decibel} expected in free field.
A log-mel baseline identifies the three small aircraft with 0.99 balanced accuracy on unseen distances, but drops to 0.78--0.97 when trained on one sensor and tested on the other, which identifies sensor shift, not aircraft identity, as the open problem these recordings can measure.
We provide a channel map, measured offsets, and leakage-aware splits so the recordings can be used without repeating these checks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{coustic} sensing is a passive, low-cost complement to radar, radio-frequency and electro-optical detection of small uncrewed aircraft systems (UAS)~\cite{taha2019review}.
Its progress depends on recordings, and most public sets were designed for other questions: arrays carried by the aircraft for source localization in ego-noise~\cite{strauss2018dregon,gupta2025droneaudioset}, fixed arrays with tracked source pose for localization~\cite{jekaterynczuk2025uavirbase}, outdoor ground arrays for noise certification~\cite{ramosromero2023dronenoise,ramosromero2023ast}, or short single-channel clips aggregated for detector training~\cite{alemadi2019audio,dads,wang2025multiclass}.
Two properties are rarely available together: (i) the same flight captured by two different microphone chains, which is what is needed to measure how a model trained on one sensor transfers to another; and (ii) enough documentation of the files to know which channel is which, how the sensors are aligned, and which segments are unusable.

This paper describes CiO-Drone-Acoustic~\cite{ciodata}, an indoor collection recorded at the CONCEPTIO Laboratory, ITA, in which every take is captured by a mono reference microphone and an eight-slot microphone array at the same time.
Its contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a documented release of 13 paired takes (\SI{3165.7}{\second} raw reference audio) of four DJI aircraft, with English file layout, sidecars and a catalog (Sections~\ref{sec:acq}--\ref{sec:records});
\item a technical validation that re-measures sensor alignment, clock drift, array channel layout, reference-channel dropouts and level against distance directly from the audio, and finds four mis-aligned pairs and a rotating channel order that the file metadata do not reveal (Section~\ref{sec:validation});
\item a reference baseline with leakage-aware protocols, including cross-sensor evaluation, and a usage recipe (Sections~\ref{sec:baseline}--\ref{sec:usage}).
\end{enumerate}
All numbers in this paper are produced by the scripts in the companion repository from the public Zenodo files.

\section{Data acquisition}\label{sec:acq}
\subsection{Sensors}
The \emph{reference} is a single Behringer channel recorded at \SI{44.1}{\kilo\hertz}; samples are stored as IEEE float32 (WAVE format~3) but lie on the 16-bit grid ($k/32768$), i.e.\ the effective resolution is 16 bits.
The \emph{array} is a ReSpeaker six-microphone circular array recorded as eight slots at \SI{16}{\kilo\hertz}, 16-bit PCM; in this kit the first six input slots are microphones and the last two are playback-echo channels~\cite{seeedwiki6mic}.
\authornote{State the Behringer interface and microphone models, the ReSpeaker kit/driver version and host, microphone mounting height, the separation between the reference microphone and the array, and the array orientation.}
Gains recorded in every sidecar are eight ones.

\subsection{Venue and protocol}
All takes were flown indoors at the CONCEPTIO Laboratory.
Twelve takes were recorded on 22~May~2026 and the Matrice~350 take on 16~September~2026.
\authornote{Give room dimensions and surface materials (or a reverberation time), the position of the sensors in the room, the pilot's instructions for the fixed-distance takes (hover vs.\ manoeuvre, height), and what ``free'' flight comprised.}
The fixed-distance takes are labelled 2, 5 and \SI{10}{\meter} in the sidecar; these are nominal distances, and no range or trajectory log was recorded.
Each take starts with a metallic strike used to align the two sensors; the processing note of the collection states that a \SI{3}{\kilo\hertz} high-pass filter was used only to locate that strike and that the stored samples are unfiltered.

\subsection{Aircraft and conditions}
Table~\ref{tab:takes} lists the 13 takes.
Three aircraft (Flip, Neo~2, Mini~4 Pro) were recorded at each of the three nominal distances.
The Mini~4 Pro was also flown freely with its propeller guard removed; the Neo~2 and Mini~4 Pro were flown together; all four aircraft were flown together; and the Matrice~350 flew one pass labelled ``10m, 5m and 2m''.
\authornote{The manufacturer sheet stored with the Flip takes lists a 795\,g aircraft with DJI FPV specifications (O3 link, 140\,km/h), whereas the DJI Flip is $<$249\,g with full propeller guards, and the original sidecar read ``DJI FPV Flip''. Confirm which aircraft was flown before submission; the class name, and Table~\ref{tab:takes}, depend on it.}

\section{Data records}\label{sec:records}
The deposit~\cite{ciodata} (version 0.1, CC0) holds one zip per aircraft or flight: \texttt{flip.zip}, \texttt{neo2.zip} and \texttt{mini4pro.zip} contain folders \texttt{2m}, \texttt{5m} and \texttt{10m}; each of the four other flights is one folder.
Every take folder holds the same five files: \texttt{raw\_reference.wav}, \texttt{raw\_array.wav}, \texttt{synchronized\_reference.wav}, \texttt{synchronized\_array.wav}, and \texttt{sidecar.json} (\texttt{timestamp}, \texttt{aircraft}, \texttt{distance}, \texttt{gains}).
\texttt{catalog.csv} indexes the takes with container durations; \texttt{channel\_audit.csv} gives three-window levels of the eight array slots; \texttt{aircraft\_specifications.json} merges the manufacturer sheets in English (copied manufacturer data, not measurements).
Totals are \SI{3165.70}{\second} raw reference, \SI{3159.04}{\second} raw array, \SI{2982.16}{\second} synchronized reference and \SI{2973.53}{\second} synchronized array.
The source share additionally held one-second slices, a six-channel mono split and spectrogram images; these are exact derivatives of the synchronized audio and were not deposited.
Sidecar strings that were wrong or in Portuguese on the source share were corrected in the package (e.g.\ the Matrice sidecar read ``DJI Flip''); the catalog records each correction.

\section{Technical validation}\label{sec:validation}
All checks run on the public Zenodo files; scripts are listed in Section~\ref{sec:code}.

\subsection{Format, clipping and reference dropouts}
All 52 WAV files open with the stated rates and channel counts, and their lengths match the six-decimal durations in \texttt{catalog.csv}.
No reference sample reaches $|x|\ge0.999$ (peaks \SIrange{-5.4}{-3.4}{dBFS}) and no array sample reaches full scale (peaks \SIrange{-21.0}{-18.2}{dBFS}).
The reference contains intervals in which the signal collapses to $\le$2\,LSB while the array continues to record the aircraft at its usual level; these are recording dropouts, not silence.
In the synchronized files they total \SI{18.2}{\second} in 15 intervals for \texttt{neo2\_10m}, \SI{5.1}{\second} for the four-aircraft flight and \SI{6.7}{\second} for the Matrice pass (mostly between 547 and \SI{580}{\second}); every other take has at most \SI{0.4}{\second}.
Their start times are listed in \texttt{results/integrity.csv} and should be masked before training or evaluation.

\subsection{Array channel layout}\label{sec:layout}
We classify each array slot on its 1-s levels in the synchronized file as \emph{dead} (median $\le\SI{-70}{dBFS}$), \emph{constant} (standard deviation of 1-s levels $<\SI{0.8}{\decibel}$) or \emph{acoustic}.
Fig.~\ref{fig:layout} shows the result.
No take has more than five acoustic slots, although the kit has six microphones; twelve takes have one constant slot near \SI{-61}{dBFS}, and the remaining slots are dead.
In nine takes the pattern is a cyclic rotation of [five acoustic, one constant, two dead]; three takes (\texttt{flip\_5m}, \texttt{neo2\_2m}, \texttt{neo2\_5m}) have four acoustic slots and \texttt{mini4pro\_noguard\_free} has one.
The position of the block changes between takes.
Random rotation of the slot order between recordings is a reported fault of the driver for this array~\cite{seeedissue309}.
Two consequences follow: a slot index in the file does not identify a physical microphone, so any processing that depends on array geometry (beamforming, direction of arrival) must first recover the mapping for each take; and the six-channel mono export of the source share, which kept slots 1--6, dropped acoustic slots 7--8 in eight takes and kept dead slots.
The channel map in \texttt{results/array\_layout.csv} replaces that export.
The three-window audit deposited with v0.1 agrees with this classification on all acoustic slots; its level ranges come from single 2-s windows and should not be read as a flight profile.

\subsection{Reference--array alignment}\label{sec:sync}
The catalog's $\Delta_\text{cont}$ is a difference of container lengths, not a measured misalignment.
To measure alignment we resample the synchronized reference to \SI{16}{\kilo\hertz}, band-pass both signals to \SIrange{200}{4000}{\hertz}, and locate a 4-s reference window inside a \SI{34}{\second} window of the first acoustic array slot by normalized cross-correlation, every \SI{10}{\second}.
Windows whose peak is below eight times the median $|\rho|$, or more than \SI{0.5}{\second} from the take median (false peaks at harmonic periods or dropouts), are discarded (7 of 256).
A Theil--Sen fit gives the offset and the relative drift (Table~\ref{tab:takes}, Fig.~\ref{fig:sync}).

In nine pairs the median offset is within \SI{65}{\milli\second}.
Four pairs are not: \texttt{flip\_5m} (\SI{-2.106}{\second}), \texttt{neo2\_5m} (\SI{+1.902}{\second}), \texttt{mini4pro\_5m} (\SI{+0.400}{\second}) and \texttt{mini4pro\_noguard\_free} (\SI{-3.589}{\second}).
These are the four pairs whose container lengths differ by more than a second, but neither the sign nor the size of $\Delta_\text{cont}$ predicts the offset (e.g.\ \SI{+10.56}{\second} versus \SI{+0.40}{\second} for \texttt{mini4pro\_5m}); most of the length difference is in how far one file runs past the other.
Twelve of the thirteen fits show a positive relative drift between \SI{178}{} and \SI{368}{ppm} (median \SI{244}{ppm}), which accumulates to \SIrange{30}{65}{\milli\second} over a three-minute take and about \SI{130}{\milli\second} over the Matrice pass; the alignment strike fixes only the start.
The per-window residual (median absolute deviation \SIrange{1}{42}{\milli\second}) is limited by the low waveform similarity of the two chains ($|\rho|\approx0.05$--$0.3$) and by the periodicity of rotor harmonics, so these figures are an upper bound on the attainable precision, not a sample-accurate alignment.

\subsection{Acoustic content}
Fig.~\ref{fig:spec} shows one minute of each aircraft on the reference.
The three small aircraft are dominated by rotor harmonics whose frequency moves with manoeuvre; the Matrice~350 is broadband with a low fundamental.
Long-term spectra (Fig.~\ref{fig:ltas}) separate the aircraft: the Flip's dominant component lies near \SI{360}{\hertz} with a second line near \SI{2}{\kilo\hertz}, the Neo~2's near \SI{1.07}{\kilo\hertz}, the Mini~4 Pro alternates between components near 280 and \SI{490}{\hertz}, and the Matrice shows a fundamental near \SI{100}{\hertz} with harmonics.
The dominant frequency of each aircraft is stable across its three distance takes (Table~\ref{tab:takes}).
Stationary tones at 26, 51 and \SI{121}{\hertz} appear in the long-term spectra of every take; \SI{121}{\hertz} is consistent with the second harmonic of the \SI{60}{\hertz} mains, so these are building noise, not aircraft signal.
A narrow tone at \SI{2.43}{\kilo\hertz} appears only in \texttt{mini4pro\_5m}.

\paragraph*{Level versus nominal distance}
Median 1-s levels decrease monotonically with nominal distance for each aircraft on both sensors (Fig.~\ref{fig:level}): on the reference by \SI{4.2}{}, \SI{5.3}{} and \SI{3.9}{\decibel} from 2 to \SI{10}{\meter} for Flip, Neo~2 and Mini~4 Pro, and on the array by \SIrange{2.3}{2.6}{\decibel}.
Spherical spreading would predict \SI{14}{\decibel}.
The small slope is what a reverberant room produces beyond its critical distance, and it may also reflect differences between the nominal and actual distance during manoeuvre.
Distance in this release is therefore a label of the protocol, not a controlled acoustic variable, and a model that generalizes across the three distances has not been shown to generalize across range.

\section{Reference baseline}\label{sec:baseline}
\subsection{Task and features}
We identify the aircraft among the three that were recorded at fixed distances (Flip, Neo~2, Mini~4 Pro) from 1-s segments.
Both sensors are brought to \SI{16}{\kilo\hertz}; for the array we use the first acoustic slot of the channel map, i.e.\ a single microphone.
Each segment is described by the mean and standard deviation over frames of 64 log-mel energies (\SIrange{50}{8000}{\hertz}, 1024-point FFT, hop 512) after subtracting the segment's mean log energy, so absolute level, which varies with distance and gain, is not available to the classifier.
The first and last \SI{5}{\second} of each take and reference-dropout seconds are excluded, giving 1528 reference and 1531 array segments.
Classifiers are an $\ell_2$-regularized logistic regression ($C=0.1$, standardized features) and a 300-tree random forest.

\subsection{Protocols}
\emph{Random}: 5-fold split over segments, ignoring take identity. Adjacent seconds of one take fall in both training and test, which is the leakage pattern documented across ML-based science~\cite{kapoor2023leakage}; we report it only for contrast.
\emph{LODO}: leave one nominal distance out; train on two takes per aircraft and test on the unseen third.
\emph{Cross-sensor}: LODO, training on one sensor and testing on the other.

\subsection{Results}
Table~\ref{tab:baseline} summarizes balanced accuracy.
Within one sensor, identification is nearly saturated even on unseen takes (0.989--0.998), because the three aircraft occupy distinct spectral regions (Fig.~\ref{fig:ltas}).
The random split therefore does not inflate results much here, but this is a property of this closed set and of a single recording session, not evidence that segment-level splits are safe.
Changing the sensor costs between 3 and 22 points, and the direction of transfer that suffers depends on the model, so the gap is not a fixed property of one microphone being ``better''.
Applied without retraining to the free flights, the logistic models labelled every second of the unguarded Mini~4 Pro flight as Mini~4 Pro on both sensors.
On the two-aircraft flight they predicted Mini~4 Pro for 89--91\,\% and Neo~2 for 9--11\,\% of seconds; the multi-aircraft takes have no time annotation, so this shows only that a single-label model reports the dominant aircraft.

\section{Usage notes}\label{sec:usage}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Select array channels from \texttt{results/array\_layout.csv}, not by slot index. Do not use the six-channel export of the source share.
\item Shift the array by $\tau$ and, for analyses that need better than \SI{50}{\milli\second}, resample it by $\dot\tau$ (Table~\ref{tab:takes}, \texttt{results/sync\_summary.csv}). Do not use $\Delta_\text{cont}$ or one-second slice indices as a shared time base.
\item Mask reference dropouts (\texttt{results/integrity.csv}).
\item Split by take. For three-class identification, use leave-one-distance-out; for sensor robustness, train and test on different sensors.
\item Treat distance as a protocol label, and the multi-aircraft takes as unlabeled in time.
\item The release contains no noise-only recording, so detection (UAS present versus absent) cannot be evaluated on it alone; combine it with an external background set and report the source of the negatives.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}
Each condition is a single take, so take-to-take variance cannot be estimated, and LODO results confound distance with take.
All small-aircraft takes were recorded in one room on one morning; room acoustics, background and aircraft identity are therefore not independent, and models may learn session cues.
The Mini~4 Pro free flight has no guarded counterpart, so the guard is confounded with the flight pattern.
There is no range, trajectory or attitude log, no microphone coordinates, and no time annotation of the multi-aircraft flights.
The array channel order is unknown per take beyond the rotation pattern of Section~\ref{sec:layout}.

\section{Conclusion}
CiO-Drone-Acoustic provides \SI{49.7}{\minute} of indoor multirotor audio captured simultaneously by two acoustic chains.
Its validation shows that files labelled as synchronized can be seconds apart, that both chains drift by a few hundred ppm, that the array's channel order rotates between takes, and that level changes little with nominal distance in this room.
With those corrections published alongside the audio, the recordings support closed-set identification and, more distinctively, controlled measurement of cross-sensor transfer, which a within-sensor benchmark on the same data would hide.
